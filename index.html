Introduction · The problem

Where care is most needed, people are least likely to ask.

1

Stigma delays care

Contraception, STIs and sexual dysfunction are stigmatised. Anonymity and 24×7 availability are why people use chat agents, and they disclose more to them than to clinicians.

2

Help-seeking is gendered

Masculine norms of stoicism and self-reliance suppress help-seeking. For erectile and ejaculatory problems, the symptom itself reads as a failure of "manly performance".

3

Evidence comes from the lab

Most evaluations use curated question sets or short pilots. The chatbot is a black box with a benchmark score. How it is built is rarely documented.

Our three questions. RQ1: Where on the care pathway do users sit?  RQ2: How does a stage-routed, tool-gated agent hold up on live traffic?  RQ3: Does engagement differ by gender?

Methodology · Design choices

Three choices, and why we made them.

RAG

Ground it, don't generate it

  • Approved provider pages → 1,000-token chunks, 150 overlap
  • ada-002 embeddings in Qdrant; top-10 by cosine similarity
  • Source URL travels with every answer
  • Update content by URL, no retraining
अ

No translation layer

  • English, Hindi, Hinglish and romanised regional text go straight to the model
  • Reply in the user's language; stated preference added to context
  • Why: translate-first pipelines lose clinical nuance
!

Judge safety after the reply

  • Separate GPT-4.1 call reads question, answer and history
  • Returns escalate yes/no + category → human workflow
  • Why: harm builds across turns; input filters miss it
Guardrails in every reply: 40–60 words  ·  never suggests a medicine (the doctor decides)  ·  only facts from context  ·  non-judgmental  ·  user-led, never pushy
Refs [16] Himel et al. 2024 (translation) · [23] Lin et al., ToxicChat (single-input moderation)
ICAIC 2026 · Paper 381

[1:15] Reveal one card at a time.

1: "Clinical content is retrieved, not generated from the model's memory. Approved provider pages are chunked, embedded and stored in Qdrant; the ten nearest chunks come back, and the source URL travels with the answer, so every answer can be traced. Clinicians update content by URL, no retraining."

2: "We deliberately skipped a translation layer. Indian users mix English, Hindi and regional languages in Roman script. Translation-first pipelines lose clinical nuance, so the model handles the text natively and replies in the user's language."

3: "Safety is judged after the reply is written, by a separate model that sees the whole exchange. Harmful patterns build across turns, which single-message filters miss."

Guardrails: "And every reply is short, never names a medicine, sticks to facts in context, and lets the user lead."

Discussion · Limitations & next steps

What this study does not show, and what we're doing next.

Not shown

  • Clinical correctness. Judges are models, not clinicians, and may favour their own model family
  • Which component helps. No ablation of routing, gating or retrieval yet
  • Quality by language. Not stratified, so code-mixed decline is neither shown nor ruled out
  • Generalisation. One provider, one country, mostly men, no human-channel comparison

Next

  • Clinician-adjudicated subsample of a few hundred responses to calibrate the judges
  • Ablation: retrieval on vs off, then gating and routing
  • Scores broken down by language
  • Intent classifier accuracy: per-label precision and recall against human labels
ICAIC 2026 · Paper 381

[0:50] Say this confidently; it earns credibility.

"A reply can be free of hallucination, helpful and well grounded, and still be the wrong advice for that patient. Our judges are models, not clinicians, so the immediate next step is a clinician-adjudicated subsample to calibrate them."

"We also haven't isolated which component is doing the work, so an ablation is next, starting with retrieval on versus off. And we'll break quality down by language, since other work shows code-mixed text degrades."

Conclusion

Three things to take home.

1
Scope what the agent can do by where the user is, and enforce it in code, not in the prompt.
2
Evaluate continuously on live traffic, and say plainly what your judge cannot see.
3
Report by people and by conversations. One view alone misstates what the system does.
